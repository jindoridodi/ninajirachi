## Latest diagnostic evidence
- Two-wearable diagnostic received ESP-NOW packets and successfully paired packets with headings when the device was still. During an unrestricted turn, heading became invalid continuously for many seconds because gravity was unavailable. During a controlled level turn, headings were mostly valid and radio observations arrived, but a confident direction appeared only briefly. Observed bucket averages often differed by less than the existing 6 dB confidence threshold. Do not reduce the gate without checking whether it points toward the actual peer.
- The level-turn capture contains `[DEBUG-dir]`, `[DEBUG-dir-obs]`, and `[DEBUG-jump]` lines. Association observations use `observedMs headingDegrees rssi`; the direction and gravity decisions are in `firmware/src/behavior/DirectionEvidence.h`, `HeadingHistory.h`, `firmware/src/board/RuntimeInputs.h`, and `firmware/src/compass/GravityEstimator.h`.
- The physical mounting implies the original assumption that compass and IMU axes align is wrong. A speculative transform was tested only on port 1891 and then removed from the workspace; that wearable was restored to the same normal firmware as the other, preserving calibration. Do not apply that transform blindly. On a subsequent table rotation (user confirmed no lifting), raw compass X ranged from -611 to -472 (span 139), Y from -1466 to 1062 (span 2528), Z from -537 to 1947 (span 2484). This conflicts with the expected sensor-output axis corresponding to the user's reported vertical Y; printed axes, sensor sign/register settings, motion, or the mounting interpretation need independent checking. The filtered `captures/axis-check.txt` contains `mag` lines from that run. The direction of rotation was not specified and the turn was faster than intended, so it cannot determine axis signs or reliably validate a 360-degree heading by itself.
- A new controlled test was prepared but NOT run: ask the user to rotate the jump-identified wearable slowly and clockwise as viewed from above through a complete 360-degree circle over about 20 seconds, without lifting it; collect timestamped QMC raw XYZ, IMU gyro XYZ, and heading while continuously reading serial. This should establish which sensor-output axes are horizontal and whether heading progresses with the correct sign. Then test a controlled tilt to establish the remaining sign, using the physically confirmed mounting and sensor datasheet. Ask the user to avoid nearby magnetic material and hold the other wearable still. Do not infer correct axis mapping solely from PCB labels or from a single magnetic field pose.
- The heading math needs both the calibrated magnetometer vector and the gravity vector transformed into the SAME wearable/display coordinate system. Calibration records are in sensor axes; preserve them and transform after per-axis calibration. The display's front and right must match screen orientation. Add independent cardinal and tilt tests and physically compare a full turn. Different mounts would require a different mapping and calibration; the user says the current two are mounted alike.
- Full user scenario remains unresolved. Distinguish sensor availability during motion from RSSI/direction confidence. The printed TURN TO SAMPLE is correct when data are insufficient; the white square denotes this wearable. Physical accuracy is not yet verified.
