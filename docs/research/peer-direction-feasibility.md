# Peer direction feasibility

Research recorded 2026-11-17. The present hardware can attempt a playful direction guess while the wearer turns, but it does not directly measure a peer's bearing. The available two-wearable captures do not establish that the current guess is reliable. Keep the centered-eye fallback until repeated ground-truth tests demonstrate otherwise.

## What the hardware measures

- The [Waveshare board](https://docs.waveshare.com/ESP32-S3-Touch-LCD-1.28) has an onboard antenna and a QMI8658 accelerometer and gyroscope. The IMU measures motion and helps estimate tilt; it does not sense where a peer is. The [QMC5883P](https://www.qstcorp.com/en_comp_prod/QMC5883P) measures a magnetic field for compass heading, not the direction of a radio transmitter. Compass heading identifies the wearable's orientation only when its mounting, calibration, tilt compensation, and magnetic environment permit it.
- ESP-NOW provides received packet signal strength through receive control information ([Espressif ESP-NOW API](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/latest/esp32/api-reference/network/esp_now.html), [Espressif FAQ](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-faq/en/latest/esp-faq-en-master.pdf)). RSSI is one scalar measurement, not an angle or an unambiguous distance. Stronger RSSI can support a proximity heuristic but cannot prove which peer is closest.
- A rotating antenna and body may produce a direction-dependent RSSI pattern. Pairing RSSI with compass heading can exploit that pattern only if its strongest response corresponds repeatably to the peer's direction in the actual mounting and environment. An antenna's peak can be offset, broad, symmetric, or obscured; changes in the transmitting wearable, body shadowing, reflections, and motion can change the result. In indoor propagation the strongest arriving path can differ from the transmitter's direction ([NIST propagation study](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=841212)). The maximum RSSI heading is therefore a hypothesis, not a measured bearing, and a fixed correction cannot be assumed without measurements.

## Current evidence

The [direction estimator](../../firmware/src/behavior/DirectionEvidence.h) bins samples into eight 45-degree heading windows, requires at least two samples in three windows and 6 dB contrast, and falls back when evidence is stale. Those thresholds are software gates, not evidence that a confident result is accurate.

The [2026-11-17 handoff](../handoff.md) reports approximately quarter-turn heading changes during flat controlled rotation, but records two poor ground-truth results: with a peer ahead, the selected bearing was 225° against an initial heading near 28°; with a peer to the left, the selected bearing was 135° against an expected bearing near 16°. Each winning bucket had only two samples. See the [ahead capture](../handoff/2026-11-17-com4-direction-ground-truth.txt), [left capture](../handoff/2026-11-17-com4-direction-left.txt), and [fixed-turn captures](../handoff/2026-11-17-com5-fixed-turn.txt). These limited trials show incorrect outputs in those conditions; they do not identify a stable offset or quantify accuracy across conditions. Free movement also caused heading dropouts. Controlled flat rotation does not establish worn tilt accuracy.

## Alternatives and next measurement

- **Bluetooth direction finding:** Bluetooth AoA uses IQ phase differences and an antenna array at the receiver; AoD places the array at the transmitter ([Bluetooth Core architecture](https://www.bluetooth.com/wp-content/uploads/Files/Specification/HTML/Core-61/out/en/architecture%2C-change-history%2C-and-conventions/architecture.html), [Bluetooth direction-finding overview](https://www.bluetooth.com/wp-content/uploads/Files/Specification/2501_Feature_Overview_Brief_Update.pdf)). Bluetooth 5 branding alone does not establish support for the needed direction-finding functions. The board's documented onboard antenna is not such an array; this route requires suitable radio, antenna array, switching, and software, and multipath can still mislead it.
- **UWB:** Additional UWB radios can measure time-based range, but one range between two devices by itself describes a circle, not a direction. Bearing needs additional spatial information, such as an appropriate multi-antenna angle-of-arrival design or multiple reference positions and geometry. UWB's time resolution helps resolve multipath, but walls and blocked paths still degrade accuracy ([NIST UWB measurements](https://www.nist.gov/ctl/smart-connected-systems-division/indoor-localization)). It is not included in the documented board.

For the existing approach, first verify the fixed sensor-axis transform and calibrated heading while worn, including pitch and roll. Then log timestamped heading and per-packet RSSI through repeated full turns with a stationary peer at known bearings, varying peer orientation, separation, body placement, and room; repeat with both wearables. Compare predicted left/center/right and angular errors against ground truth, including wrong confident outputs and time spent in fallback. Establish whether any antenna-pattern offset repeats before tuning thresholds. This separates heading failures from RF-pattern failures and can determine whether the playful approximation is useful without claiming a precision it has not demonstrated.
